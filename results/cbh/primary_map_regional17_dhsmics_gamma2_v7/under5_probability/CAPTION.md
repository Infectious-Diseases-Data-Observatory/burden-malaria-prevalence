# Figure 4 caption

Probability of dying before age 5 in 2024 per 1,000 live births, by country (42 sub-Saharan African countries in the burden comparison): all causes, from IHME age-specific all-cause death rates (light bars), and the part caused by malaria under the PfPR-ACM model (dark bars, with 95% intervals); both values are listed on the right. Probabilities are period life-table (synthetic-cohort) calculations, 5q0 = 1 − exp(−Σ_g H_g), over the model's seven age bands (<1, 1–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, 36–47 and 48–59 completed months), with band cumulative hazards H_g from the IHME death rates. The malaria-caused probability is 5q0 minus the probability with malaria transmission removed, for which each band's hazard is multiplied by the model's hazard ratio for PfPR[2–10] = 0 versus the country's current population-weighted MAP prevalence. Intervals draw each band's log hazard ratio independently; they condition on the IHME rates and the fitted smoothing parameters. Zero prevalence lies below the observed exposure support, so the counterfactual is an extrapolation. Against UN IGME under-five mortality for 2024, the IHME-based all-cause probabilities have median ratio 1.07 and correlation 0.84; IHME and UN IGME differ markedly for some countries. The largest malaria-caused probabilities are in South Sudan (35 per 1,000), Sierra Leone (33) and Central African Republic (33); the median is 13 per 1,000, 21% of the all-cause probability.
