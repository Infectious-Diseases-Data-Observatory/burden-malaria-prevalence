## 1. Scientific questions and reporting choices

**Manuscript naming rule (code pointer):** call the proposed method **“PfPR-ACM model”** in every main-paper and supplementary figure that identifies the method, including legends, axis labels and captions. Do not use “New method”, “Our model” or “Primary MAP model” as its display name. Plotting code must source [R_cbh/reporting/labels.R](../R_cbh/reporting/labels.R) and use `cbh_paper_model_label()` rather than defining its own label. Where needed, append an exposure or sensitivity qualifier to this shared name. Preserve descriptive outcome labels and the no-title/no-subtitle convention; figures without a model-name label do not need an extra label. This is a presentation change, not a change to the model, estimates or internal data identifiers.

The primary causal question is: **if PfPR₂–₁₀ changes from X% to Y%, how does all-cause mortality in age group g change?** Estimate this separately for **<1, 1–5, 6–11, 12–23, 24–35, 36–47 and 48–59 completed months**. All seven age-specific effects, including neonatal mortality, are primary quantities of interest. Retain estimates regardless of direction or significance.

The exposure is *P. falciparum* prevalence standardized to ages 2–10 years, expressed on the 0–100 percentage scale. Regional annual prevalence is the same exposure definition for every age group. Values assigned to a child's successive bands can differ because their entry years differ. The outcome is death during a band, conditional on reaching it alive; the cloglog model estimates an annualized mortality hazard.

Report relative and absolute mortality changes for specified X%→Y% contrasts in the same target population. National malaria-attributable deaths are a **secondary extrapolation of the primary MAP model**. They additionally require assumptions about transportability, national exposure and the counterfactual intervention. Agreement with IHME, WHO or trials does not establish causal identification. The plan does not seek a null neonatal association or interpret one as proof of no confounding.

The age-band estimand conditions on reaching that band alive. An intervention beginning at birth can change who survives into later bands; conditional band effects are not automatically the total effect of a lifelong intervention. Keep this distinction explicit in causal interpretation and synthetic-cohort survival figures.

## 2. Stage 1 — make the analysis datasets

### 2.1 Inputs and provenance

Two birth-history sources pass through the same child–age-band builder. **DHS and MIS:** the active builder is [R_cbh/01_make_analysis_data.R](../R_cbh/01_make_analysis_data.R), with [configuration](../R_cbh/00_config.R) and [dataset documentation](../R_cbh/README.md); it processes authorized local DHS Births Recodes. **MICS:** the SPSS files in `data/MICS_Datasets/` are extracted to `data/MICS_extracted/` by `R_mics/00a_extract.R` and inventoried by `R_mics/00`–`03`; `R_mics/04`–`11` build boundaries, exposure, recodes, child bands and covariates ([R_mics/README.md](../R_mics/README.md); Sections 2.2–2.4). MICS microdata and derived files stay in the ignored `data/` tree; only code, reviewed configuration and aggregate results are tracked. Both sources join reviewed geography and external-data snapshots. Record source release, units, coverage, file hashes, survey versions and exclusions. Optional input retrieval is separate from local processing; do not refresh online sources because a cache is absent.

The registry holds **124 DHS/MIS surveys and 93 MICS surveys in 43 countries**. 46 MICS surveys have no complete birth history (all 24 MICS2, 12 of 16 MICS3 and 10 of 19 MICS4 surveys) and cannot support the age-band design; the other **47, in 28 countries**, have a full birth history equivalent to the DHS Births Recode ([eligibility assessment](../results/mics_inventory/ELIGIBILITY.md)). Keep every in-scope survey in the registry with a status for missing recodes, boundaries, dates, exposure or covariates. Survey-year MAP availability alone must not determine whether earlier band entries are usable. Configured entry years are 2000–2024.

### 2.2 Geography and exposure

Use survey/boundary-version-specific region keys, explicit normalization, curated synonyms and reviewed aggregation rules. Do not infer a match by elimination or accept an unreviewed fuzzy match. Keep unresolved records in the exclusion ledger. Region identifiers in both programmes include the survey/boundary version; they are not automatically longitudinal regional identities.

**MICS regions.** [R_mics/05_region_map.R](../R_mics/05_region_map.R) maps each MICS region label to an analysis region, a union of boundary polygons (`data/derived_mics/region_map.csv`: 366 labels, 291 matched by exact name and 75 by the reviewed overrides in `R_mics/config/region_overrides.csv`). DHS boundary files are reused where they exist, merging MICS regions into DHS groupings (Togo's Lomé strata, Chad's Ennedi and Borkou, Mauritania's Tiris Zemmour and Inchiri), dissolving DHS polygons (Nouakchott) or aggregating Malawi 2013 districts to its three regions; Nigeria uses the six zones, as its DHS surveys do. Guinea-Bissau and the Central African Republic have no DHS boundary: [R_mics/04_boundaries_gnb_caf.R](../R_mics/04_boundaries_gnb_caf.R) builds their polygons from a local Africa admin-1 file (outside this repository) that merges each capital with a neighbour, so Bissau is combined with Biombo (8 analysis regions instead of 9) and Bangui with Region 1 (6 instead of 7); CAR's unnamed MICS regions follow the country's seven health regions. Somalia, South Sudan and São Tomé use approximate groupings; none of these surveys enters the primary. Dakar city 2015 enters as one approximate analysis region, the combined Dakar and Thiès polygon of the admin-1 file. [R_mics/06_polygons_pfpr_registry.R](../R_mics/06_polygons_pfpr_registry.R) extracts annual regional PfPR with the DHS method.

Assign regional annual MAP PfPR₂–₁₀ from the **calendar year containing band entry**. Hold this value fixed over the band, independently of death timing. The current analysis does not average prevalence over a child's realized person-time or the full band, interpolate between years, borrow the nearest year, or carry the last exposure forward. Preserve genuine zero prevalence and distinguish it from missing exposure. `calendar_year` is fractional entry time; `entry_year` is the integer source year.

The region is the mother's residence at interview; historical migration is not reconstructed. Record coverage and geographic-source flags. The inherited DHS regional raster extracts and the MICS extraction, which reproduces the same GPW density weights without cell area and the same mask to the last raster (code-audit finding F01), both still require consolidation and validation; the cell-area correction used in national burden extraction does not validate either.

### 2.3 Child–age-band records and eligibility

Use one row per child and age band, with disjoint intervals `[0,1), [1,6), [6,12), [12,24), [24,36), [36,48), [48,60)` months. Predetermined band widths are 1/12, 5/12, 6/12, 1, 1, 1 and 1 year. The rules apply identically to DHS and MICS records.

1. The child must reach the band alive; exclude bands after death.
2. Entry must satisfy `interview_cmc - 60 <= band_entry_cmc < interview_cmc`.
3. The full potential band must end by interview, **for deaths and survivors alike**. Exclude a death in a band that would otherwise be incomplete.
4. The entry year must lie within the configured exposure period. Apply geography, exposure and selected-covariate availability rules with recorded exclusion reasons.

`death` is 1 for death during the band and 0 for survival through it. `band_years` is the full predetermined width, **not observed time until death**. The earlier aggregate Poisson/negative-binomial person-time dataset is not the primary analysis dataset.

Retain recorded death-age units/days and B6/B7 disagreement flags. The DHS month-based neonatal coding includes reported days 0–30; its boundary differs from the exact 28-day definition in the Burstein text and the IHME neonatal export. Review ambiguous intervals, impossible dates, duplicate identifiers, age heaping and calendar conversions. Older Ethiopian recodes use the declared +92 CMC approximation; ET8AFL is already Gregorian. Do not replace documented rules with generic calendar heuristics.

**MICS records.** [R_mics/07_make_recodes.R](../R_mics/07_make_recodes.R) converts each MICS birth history to the DHS layout: survival (MICS 1/2 to DHS 1/0), twins, age at death (units and number to the DHS three-digit code) and weights. [R_mics/08_build_child_bands.R](../R_mics/08_build_child_bands.R) runs the unchanged builder into `data/derived_mics/cbh_build/`, overriding paths in memory so the DHS manifest and shards are untouched. Of 1,504,399 MICS children, 1,503,036 pass the validity checks; under 1% fail in every survey except Somalia 2006 (3.9%, mostly invalid dates or unknown survival). The five Lesotho surveys (LS41FL, LS61FL, LS71FL, LS81FL, MC_LSO2018) have no MAP surface and are not processed, leaving 166 built surveys (120 DHS/MIS, 46 MICS) in 40 countries.

Validate synthetic birth histories, age boundaries, five-year eligibility, complete-band selection, annual joins and exclusion accounting. Preserve survey, mother, cluster and stratum identifiers under the ignored data directory for dependence/design checks. [Build validation](../R_cbh/BUILD_VALIDATION.md) and [open issues](OPEN_ANALYSIS_ISSUES.md) record current evidence and limitations.

### 2.4 Covariates, HIV imputation and missing data

Select contextual predictors of mortality and higher PfPR at the subnational level rather than individual birth-composition predictors. All survey-derived predictors (DHS recodes or published DHS indicators; MICS microdata) are **survey-region summaries**, with no individual child, mother or household predictor in the primary model. Exclude sex composition, multiple births and birth order from both adjustment and complete-case requirements. Use regional mean maternal age **at first birth**, not average maternal age at each birth. Include regional wasting and stunting prevalence as two separate covariates. Retain urban residence, education, wealth, DTP3/measles, facility delivery, short birth interval, WASH and electricity. Hib3, PCV, rotavirus, exclusive breastfeeding and underweight remain excluded. The four national annual measures are retained where regional data are unavailable; they must not be described as subnational measurements. The outcome remains a child–age-band observation.

The intended role is adjustment for regional conditions associated with mortality and transmission, not selection by statistical significance or an established claim that every covariate causes higher PfPR. Nutritional prevalence is included by decision, but it may also reflect consequences of malaria. Survey-time anthropometry in surviving children cannot by itself establish that nutrition preceded the historical exposure or death. Record this possible mediation/selection issue and compare the primary model with a model omitting both nutritional measures on the same analysis sample (Section 3.4.4). Regional aggregation does not establish a sufficient causal adjustment set. Definition sources are the [DHS recode manual (`v212`)](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/DHSG4/Recode7_DHS_10Sep2018_DHSG4.pdf), [DHS nutritional-status indicators](https://www.dhsprogram.com/data/Indicator-Explorer.cfm?indgrpid=194) and [DHS tabulation plan](https://dhsprogram.com/pubs/pdf/DHSM6/Final_Report_Tab_Plan_24Oct2014_DHSM6.pdf); they do not establish data availability for each eligible survey.

**MICS derivations.** MICS publishes no regional indicator tables, so [R_mics/09_regional_covariates.R](../R_mics/09_regional_covariates.R) computes all 13 regional covariates from the microdata with the definitions above. Documented differences: education is recorded as level and grade and is converted to completed years with each country's primary and secondary durations, counting women who never attended school as zero; facility delivery covers only the last birth in the two years before interview; anthropometry uses the WHO-standard z-scores (`HAZ2`/`WHZ2`, or `who_z.sav` for MICS3), never the NCHS versions; household water and sanitation follow the JMP 2017 classification; MICS6 off-grid electricity counts as electricity; age at first birth and short birth intervals come from the birth history. Weights are `wmweight` for women, `hhweight` (or `hhweightMICS`) for households and `chweight` (or `chweightMICS`) for children. The survey random intercept can absorb a survey-wide shift in the linear predictor, including one caused by a definition difference that is constant across a survey's regions, but not a definition difference that varies between regions within a survey or alters a covariate's association with mortality.

For DHS recode-derived means use women's sampling weights (`v005/1e6`) and the denominator above, calculated **before exposure/outcome complete-case selection**; MICS means use the weights just listed on the same denominators. Never average child-band rows, which would weight surviving children repeatedly. Published household indicators use their source's household denominator and weighting; do not approximate these using women’s weights on deduplicated birth histories. For wasting/stunting, preserve the survey's anthropometry population, age coverage, reference standard and weighting; do not use birth-history rows as the denominator or require a mortality child's own anthropometric measurement. Audit availability and measurement flags before adopting a published estimate; do not silently combine older growth references or narrower age ranges with WHO-standard 0–59-month estimates. If reconstruction is needed, use the appropriate anthropometry recode, valid WHO z-scores and its sampling weights. Missing measurements must not be treated as absence of malnutrition. Preserve the region key's survey/boundary version. National annual variables remain constant across regions within a country-year because no regional source is available; assigning them to a region does not create regional variation.

All 17 scalar covariates (13 regional and four national) enter linearly after centering/scaling on the complete-case modelling sample of each version (v7: DHS and MICS with Liberia, `covariate_scaling.csv`). Mean wealth-quintile score is a numerical regional summary, not the former five-level individual factor. Save new scaling in a new analysis version; do not reuse another version's scaling. Within each separately fitted age band, regional covariates have their own coefficients. PfPR and fractional calendar time remain band-entry exposure/time variables rather than being averaged over children.

Survey-region coverage, wealth, urban residence and education describe survey-time conditions or the indicator's stated retrospective window. Do not relabel them as reconstructed historical values at band entry. **Urban/rural residence is required as `urban_pct`, the weighted percentage urban among distinct interviewed mothers with a recent birth in the region.** DTP3 and measles are the only selected vaccine coverage measures. Preserve the excluded vaccine/breastfeeding extraction history, but neither their missingness nor pre-introduction assumptions affect primary eligibility. Maternal death fraction and hypothetical children remain outside the adjustment set.

**National UNICEF vaccination imputation:** retain every available regional DTP3/measles value; fill only unavailable values with a valid national WUENIC percentage for the **same survey year**. This timing matches those survey-time regional measures. Preserve genuine reported zeros. This is deterministic national substitution, not a fitted imputation model. For MICS Guinea 2016, Comoros 2022 and Chad 2019, whose recall vaccination doses are unusable, all regional DTP3 and measles values are replaced by the national WUENIC estimate for the survey year (user decision), with the same flags; the trigger there is data quality, not missingness. The historical national Hib3/PCV/rotavirus series and pre-rollout zero scenarios are retained for provenance only and are not used by the current primary adjustment set.

Save the value before substitution, an imputation indicator, national source year and source category for every vaccine. Substitutions remain national values assigned to survey-regions; they do not reconstruct within-country differences. Imputation flags describe provenance and do not automatically add predictor terms. Uncertainty in national estimates or regional departures from them is not propagated by this single substitution. Include an observed-regional-only/reduced-adjustment sensitivity to assess dependence on the fallback. Remaining regional gaps follow the available-region rule below; retained national annual gaps remain missing after the existing HIV imputation.

**Regional missingness fallback:** after the DTP3/measles national fallback, substitute each missing regional covariate with the arithmetic mean of finite available regions in the **same survey**, separately for each variable; R_mics/09 applies the same rule to MICS. Count each donor region once, not once per child-band record. Preserve all observed values and store original values, imputation flags, donor counts and source labels. Missing individual responses are already excluded from within-region weighted means and must not propagate into an otherwise estimable aggregate. If no usable donor region exists in that survey, leave the value missing; do not borrow across surveys or apply regional means to national country-year variables. This carries forward the user-specified within-survey imputation approach; its uncertainty is not propagated. Retain a no-regional-substitution sensitivity.

They exclude 1,299,504 MAP-eligible records (14.8%) and 34 whole surveys (25 DHS/MIS, including all five MIS, and 9 MICS) from v5, and 1,190,841 records and 31 whole surveys (22 DHS/MIS and 9 MICS) from v7, which admits Liberia's three DHS surveys. ## 3. Stage 2 — primary and sensitivity analyses

### 3.1 Primary working model

**Separate age-band MAP models with gamma=2 and the 17-variable regional adjustment.** This is the fitted specification of `primary_map_regional17_dhsmics_gamma2_v7`; `results/cbh/primary_map_regional17_dhsmics_gamma2_v7/model_formula.txt` records the formula as fitted. The formula is identical to that of v5 and the DHS-only v3; confounders are re-scaled on each version's sample (`covariate_scaling.csv`). For each of the seven bands, fit:

```r
form <- death ~
  s(pfpr_pct, bs = "cr", k = 5) +
  s(calendar_year, bs = "cr", k = 6) +
  z_mean_maternal_age_first_birth + z_mean_maternal_education_years +
  z_mean_wealth_quintile + z_urban_pct +
  z_dtp3_pct + z_measles_pct + z_facility_delivery_pct +
  z_short_birth_interval_pct + z_improved_water_pct +
  z_improved_sanitation_pct + z_electricity_pct +
  z_wasting_pct + z_stunting_pct +
  z_log_hiv_incidence + z_log_gdp_pc +
  z_log_health_expenditure_pc + z_political_stability +
  s(survey, bs = "re") + s(country, bs = "re") +
  s(region, bs = "re") + offset(log(band_years))

# d_g: DHS and MICS regional-adjustment complete cases for this one age band.
# knots_g: saved PfPR and calendar-year knots from the MAP reference fit.
fit_g <- mgcv::bam(
  form, data = d_g, knots = knots_g,
  family = binomial(link = "cloglog"),
  method = "fREML", discrete = TRUE,
  gamma = 2, select = FALSE,
  na.action = na.fail, control = mgcv::gam.control(maxit = 100)
)
```

For child i and band g, with width Δg in years:

\[
D_{ig}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(q_{ig}),\qquad
q_{ig}=1-\exp(-\Delta_g\lambda_{ig}),
\]

\[
\log\lambda_{ig}=\alpha_g+f_g(P_{r(i),y_{ig}})+h_g(t_{ig})+
\mathbf X_{r(i),s(i),y_{ig}}^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\beta_g+
u_{s(i),g}+v_{c(i),g}+b_{r(i),g}.
\]

Here X contains survey-region summaries from Section 2.4 and national annual covariates assigned to those regions; it contains no individual confounding predictor. P is regional annual MAP PfPR₂–₁₀, y is band-entry year, and t is fractional calendar year at entry. The exposure surface has no age-band-specific definition; the function f and the entry year depend on the band. Equivalently, `cloglog(q_ig) = log(Δg) + log(λ_ig)`.

Each fit estimates its own intercept, PfPR and time curves, confounder coefficients and survey/country/region random intercepts. Within each model, the random effects are independent zero-mean Gaussians, with a separate estimated variance for each grouping type. No time spline, random intercept or variance parameter is shared across age bands. Separate fitting does not imply independent sampling errors across ages because children and surveys contribute to multiple bands.

`gamma = 2` changes smoothing selection throughout the model, including time and random effects; it is not a PfPR-only multiplier. The primary PfPR basis remains **cr**, not cs. No monotonicity constraint is imposed. Reuse the reference MAP knots for these promoted fits; they are recorded and verified in the fitting pipeline.

The unweighted likelihood, fixed HIV imputation and conditional model covariance are current working choices. Address survey design, residual dependence and imputation/exposure uncertainty before claiming comprehensive uncertainty. The primary decision follows review of results and user instruction; it does not establish superior causal identification or formal model-selection evidence.

**Executable formula/data specification:** [primary/specification.R](../R_cbh/primary/specification.R) defines `cbh_primary_regional_spec()` and `cbh_primary_regional_formula()`, which return the 13 regional and four annual covariates above when `settings$nutrition` is true. [primary/settings.R](../R_cbh/primary/settings.R) maps `regional_mics` (the default of the [regional runner](../R_cbh/primary/run_regional.R) and the reporting scripts) to v7 (since 24 September 2026), `regional_mics_v5` to the v5 history (no runner flag), `regional` (`--dhs-only`) to v3, `regional18` to the 18-variable benchmark and `regional_imputed` (`--imputed`) to v4; v8 (like v6 before it) runs from `R_cbh/sensitivity/imputation_dhsmics/` so that the primary's code provenance is untouched. [covariates/regional.R](../R_cbh/covariates/regional.R) keeps the older `cbh_regional_spec()` (18 variables) for the benchmark and the `cbh_regional_mean_fill()` fallback; [covariates/unicef.R](../R_cbh/covariates/unicef.R) implements national vaccination substitution. The committed [reference knots](../R_cbh/primary/reference_knots.csv) are unchanged. ### 3.2 Effect summaries

For each of the **seven** age groups and any specified X%→Y% contrast:

\[
HR_g(X\to Y)=\exp\{f_g(Y)-f_g(X)\},\qquad
\%\Delta_g=100[HR_g(X\to Y)-1].
\]

Absolute changes require an explicit target population or calibrated baseline: `rate_g(Y) - rate_g(X)`. Express rates with declared person-year units. For band death probabilities use `q = 1 - exp(-Δg × rate)`; a hazard ratio is not a death-probability ratio. Random effects and other additive predictors cancel in a within-target hazard ratio, but not in marginal absolute-rate standardization.

Report full curves, 40%→20% contrasts and exposure support; additional AF anchors at 10%, 30% and 50% remain useful. For reference p0, `AF_g(p,p0) = 1 - exp[f_g(p0)-f_g(p)]`. Keep the zero reference explicit, assess a 1% reference as a sensitivity, and flag extrapolation. Retain signed effects and intervals, including negative values. The current CBH burden calculation does not truncate negative contributions.

Age-band intervals currently condition on smoothing parameters, one HIV imputation and point-estimate exposure. Do not sum marginal confidence limits or assume independent age-band errors to construct national intervals. Future uncertainty propagation should reuse common survey/design replicates and HIV trajectories across bands and countries before aggregating.

### 3.3 Diagnostics and validation

Check convergence, coefficient rank, finite covariance, smoothing gradients/Hessian, fitted outcomes, residual patterns, exposure support and influential surveys/countries. Verify every stored outcome, predictor and offset against intended inputs. Check compact spline contrasts/variances against full prediction matrices, including zero-PfPR extrapolation. Keep exact model hashes, sample definitions, knots, software versions and warning records.

Numerically flagged fits may receive a tighter-tolerance restart under the **same gamma and specification**; record original and selected attempts. Do not use fREML values to rank models across different gamma values or exposure sources. No v5, v6, v7 or v8 point fit needed a restart (smallest v5 smoothing-Hessian eigenvalue 7.4e-05, 48–59 months); restarts were selected in the v2 subgroup refits (Sahel and Eastern Africa 48–59 months, late-period 12–23 months), the v2 no-nutrition refit (48–59 months) and the DHS-only v3 (6–11 and 48–59 months). Use survey-grouped cross-validation for flexible-structure comparisons and country-held-out validation for transportability. Keep all records from each survey together. Examine within-region versus between-region exposure information; the existing coding does not itself identify a within-region causal effect. Assess smoothing-uncertainty-corrected covariance when available and record when it is unavailable. Bayesian sampling checks apply only if a Bayesian extension is undertaken, not as requirements for the selected bam fit.

### 3.4.5 SMC introduction before/after (added 23 September 2026)

**Status (24 September 2026):** kept as a supplementary result; it is not part of the main analysis or the paper, and its figures are not exported to the manuscript. Refitted on the v7 primary as `results/cbh/smc_dhsmics_map_gamma2_v2/` ([report](../results/cbh/smc_dhsmics_map_gamma2_v2/REPORT.md)); the sample figures and results below are those of v1 on v5.

**Request and design.** Characterise the association of seasonal malaria chemoprevention (SMC) introduction with all-cause mortality by age: the seven primary age-band models (formula, 17 covariates with the full-sample scaling, reference knots, gamma=2, random effects) refitted in the countries that have introduced SMC, with one extra term, an admin-1 indicator equal to 1 when the band entry year is at or after the unit's SMC switch-on year. Code: [R_cbh/sensitivity/smc/](../R_cbh/sensitivity/smc/settings.R) (01 assignment, 02 fits, 03 report, 04 maps).

**Switch-on years.** Source: `data/SMC_rollout/smc_by_dhs_cluster.csv` (GiveWell DataWell; DHS/MIS clusters in ten countries; licensed cluster identifiers stay in the ignored data folder). Admin-1 units are the analysis regions, except in Nigeria, whose analysis regions are the six zones; there the unit is the state, taken for each child from its survey recode row (psu codes re-derived and checked against the shards). The zone remains the unit of PfPR, covariates and the survey-region random intercept. A unit's switch-on year is the first year by which at least half of its clusters, pooled over rounds, lay in areas with SMC (unmatched clusters dropped; never-covered clusters count as never treated).

**Main analysis: confirmed status only (decided 23 September 2026).** The codebook states that national-scope switch-on years are not a treatment flag. The main analysis uses district- or region-confirmed switch-ons (Nigeria, Burkina Faso, Uganda, Cameroon North and Far North); records of units known only from national-scope records (Mali, Niger, Chad, Côte d'Ivoire, the rest of Cameroon) are kept while no campaign had been recorded in the unit and dropped from the first recorded campaign year. Sample: 2,019,244 records, 36,140 deaths, 158,235 post-SMC records (2,246 deaths); 24 admin-1 units switch within a survey (Burkina Faso 2, Cameroon 2, Nigeria 20). Côte d'Ivoire, Niger and Uganda have no bands entered after their switch-on. Variants: the same sample without the SMC term (PfPR curves); a placebo with the switch-on 3 years early in pre-switch-on records; national-scope years taken as switch-ons (sensitivity; 2,287,670 records, 75 switching units); the share of confirmed clusters covered in place of the indicator (sensitivity).

**Results.** No age band shows a statistically detectable association. Main hazard ratios, after versus before switch-on: 1.05 (0.95–1.17) at <1 month, 1.15 (0.96–1.39) at 1–5, 0.85 (0.70–1.04) at 6–11, 0.94 (0.77–1.13) at 12–23, 0.93 (0.77–1.11) at 24–35, 1.06 (0.85–1.31) at 36–47 and 0.83 (0.61–1.12) at 48–59 months. The placebo hazard ratios lie between 0.92 and 1.07, none significant. The <1 and 1–5 month bands, largely outside SMC eligibility (from 3 months), serve as negative controls. The models adjust for PfPR at entry, so estimates exclude any effect mediated through MAP prevalence; they are descriptive associations, not causal effects. [Report](../results/cbh/smc_dhsmics_map_gamma2_v1/REPORT.md), [hazard ratios](../results/cbh/smc_dhsmics_map_gamma2_v1/smc_effects.csv), [serial coverage maps 2015–2022](../results/cbh/smc_dhsmics_map_gamma2_v1/smc_coverage_maps_2015_2022.png).

**Limits.** Other v5 countries with SMC programmes are not in the cluster file (Benin, Ghana, Guinea, The Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique, Mauritania, Senegal, Togo: 1,779,094 records, 20,931 deaths); the codebook names Mozambique's unmerged district records. Survey-region random intercepts are near zero in some bands, so comparisons between units and surveys also inform the SMC term. Entry-year timing codes 3,738 post-SMC records in the <1 to 6–11 month bands as post although they end before the first campaign season.

1. `Rscript run_all.R --check-inputs` checks local source prerequisites.
2. `Rscript run_all.R --mics-build` extracts the SPSS files (`R_mics/00a_extract.R`) and runs `R_mics/00`–`11` ([R_mics/README.md](../R_mics/README.md)).
