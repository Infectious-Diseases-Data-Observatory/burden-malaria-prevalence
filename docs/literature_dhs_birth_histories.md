# Analysing DHS birth-history data: a verified reading list

Compiled 2026-09-04 from a five-angle search (small-area estimation, data quality, survival modelling, exposure linkage, global estimation).
Every entry was checked against Crossref or PubMed for title, journal, year and DOI; 15 candidates were dropped as off-topic or unverifiable.
Tiers are a reviewer's grading of methodological content, not of the journal.

## Core methodological papers

Statistics and methods papers whose subject is how to analyse birth-history data.

- **Trussell et al. (1983)**, *Demography*. A hazards-model analysis of the covariates of infant and child mortality in Sri Lanka. [doi:10.2307/2060898](https://doi.org/10.2307/2060898)  
  Applies piecewise-constant proportional-hazards models to WFS birth-history data, splitting each child's exposure into age segments with right-censoring at interview and estimating covariate effects by age segment.
- **Guo et al. (1992)**, *Journal of the American Statistical Association*. Estimating a Multivariate Proportional Hazards Model for Clustered Data Using the EM Algorithm, with an Application to Child Survival in Guatemala. [doi:10.1080/01621459.1992.10476251](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1992.10476251)  
  Introduces a shared-frailty proportional-hazards model for children clustered within mothers, fitted by EM with piecewise-exponential hazards on birth-history data, and shows how ignoring within-family dependence distorts standard errors and covariate effects.
- **Sastry et al. (1997)**, *Journal of the American Statistical Association*. A Nested Frailty Model for Survival Data, with an Application to the Study of Child Survival in Northeast Brazil. [doi:10.1080/01621459.1997.10473994](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1997.10473994)  
  Extends frailty survival models to two nested levels (family within community) with piecewise-exponential baseline hazards, fitted to DHS birth-history data from Brazil, and partitions unobserved heterogeneity between the two levels.
- **Korenromp et al. (2004)**, *International Journal of Epidemiology*. Monitoring trends in under-5 mortality rates through national birth history surveys. [doi:10.1093/ije/dyh182](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyh182)  
  Quantifies the sampling error of U5MR estimates from 41 African DHS birth-history surveys (median relative error about 4%) and assesses the power of successive surveys to detect trends, with malaria-control monitoring as motivation.
- **Adebayo et al. (2005)**, *Statistics in Medicine*. Analysing child mortality in Nigeria with geoadditive discrete-time survival models. [doi:10.1002/sim.1842](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.1842)  
  Fits Bayesian geoadditive discrete-time survival models to Nigeria 1999 DHS birth histories (child-month hazards with P-spline baseline, nonlinear covariate smooths and structured spatial random effects) via MCMC.
- **Rajaratnam et al. (2010)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Measuring Under-Five Mortality: Validation of New Low-Cost Methods. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1000253](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000253)  
  Uses 166 DHS full birth histories as a validation set to develop and test new summary-birth-history estimators (maternal-age, time-since-first-birth and combined cohort methods), showing they outperform the classical Brass indirect approach against direct full-birth-history estimates.
- **Pedersen et al. (2012)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Child Mortality Estimation: Appropriate Time Periods for Child Mortality Estimates from Full Birth Histories. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001289](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001289)  
  Uses DHS data and simulation to study how the reference-period length for synthetic-cohort estimates from full birth histories trades sampling variance against truncation and recall bias, and recommends period lengths for national and subnational estimation.
- **Dwyer-Lindgren et al. (2013)**, *Population Health Metrics*. Error and bias in under-5 mortality estimates derived from birth histories with small sample sizes. [doi:10.1186/1478-7954-11-13](https://doi.org/10.1186/1478-7954-11-13)  
  Uses simulation from full birth histories to quantify how error and bias in period U5MR estimates grow as the number of women (or births) in a subsample shrinks, with implications for subnational and short-window estimation.
- **Alkema et al. (2014)**, *The Annals of Applied Statistics*. Global estimation of child mortality using a Bayesian B-spline Bias-reduction model. [doi:10.1214/14-aoas768](https://doi.org/10.1214/14-aoas768)  
  Develops the UN IGME B3 model: national U5MR trends as penalised B-splines with a data model in which each survey series (including DHS birth-history estimates) carries series-specific bias and observation-specific variance, including handling of retrospective recall and reference-period effects.
- **Mercer et al. (2015)**, *The Annals of Applied Statistics*. Space-time smoothing of complex survey data: Small area estimation for child mortality. [doi:10.1214/15-aoas872](https://doi.org/10.1214/15-aoas872)  
  Introduces the two-stage 'smoothed direct' approach: survey-weighted discrete-hazard U5MR estimates with design-based (jackknife) variances for each Tanzanian region and period, then smoothed with a Bayesian space-time model (ICAR spatial, random-walk temporal) fitted in INLA.
- **Alexander et al. (2018)**, *Demographic Research*. Global estimation of neonatal mortality using a Bayesian hierarchical splines regression model. [doi:10.4054/demres.2018.38.15](https://doi.org/10.4054/demres.2018.38.15)  
  Models the neonatal-to-under-five mortality ratio with a Bayesian hierarchical splines regression using survey- and VR-derived inputs, with source-type-specific bias and variance terms, to produce national neonatal mortality series.
- **Burstein et al. (2018)**, *PLOS Medicine*. Development and validation of a new method for indirect estimation of neonatal, infant, and child mortality trends using summary birth histories. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1002687](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002687)  
  Fits a discrete-hazards generalised additive model with hierarchical country-year effects to full birth histories from 243 DHS surveys (about 8.5 million children) and uses it to predict age-specific mortality trends from summary birth histories, with out-of-sample validation.
- **Wakefield et al. (2019)**, *Statistical Methods in Medical Research*. Estimating under-five mortality in space and time in a developing world context. [doi:10.1177/0962280218767988](https://doi.org/10.1177/0962280218767988)  
  Compares area-level smoothed-direct estimation with unit-level discrete-hazards models fitted to individual birth-month records (age-band-specific logistic hazards, cluster and survey random effects), discussing complex design, HIV bias adjustment and benchmarking, with a Kenya admin-1 application.
- **Godwin et al. (2021)**, *Statistics in Medicine*. Space-time modeling of child mortality at the Admin-2 level in a low and middle income countries context. [doi:10.1002/sim.8854](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8854)  
  Develops cluster-level Bayesian discrete-hazard models for U5MR at admin-2 with separate neonatal and post-neonatal hazards, urban/rural stratum effects, and BYM2 spatial plus RW2 temporal random effects, comparing them with smoothed-direct estimates under sparse data.
- **Wilson et al. (2021)**, *Biometrics*. Child mortality estimation incorporating summary birth history data. [doi:10.1111/biom.13383](https://doi.org/10.1111/biom.13383)  
  Develops a joint Bayesian space-time discrete-hazards model in which full birth histories enter as age-period hazard likelihoods and summary birth histories (children ever born/surviving) enter through a likelihood derived from the same hazards, so both data types inform one set of age-specific mortality rates.
- **Paige et al. (2022)**, *Journal of Survey Statistics and Methodology*. Design- and Model-Based Approaches to Small-Area Estimation in a Low- and Middle-Income Country Context: Comparisons and Recommendations. [doi:10.1093/jssam/smaa011](https://doi.org/10.1093/jssam/smaa011)  
  Uses simulation and Kenya DHS data to compare design-based, area-level and unit-level geostatistical models for DHS-type outcomes, quantifying bias from ignoring the stratified two-stage cluster design and urban/rural oversampling.
- **Altay et al. (2024)**, *Statistical Modelling*. Impact of jittering on raster- and distance-based geostatistical analyses of DHS data. [doi:10.1177/1471082X231219847](https://doi.org/10.1177/1471082X231219847)  
  Develops a geostatistical model that integrates over the DHS cluster-displacement (jittering) distribution for both the spatial random field and raster- or distance-derived covariates (GeoAdjust R package), and shows in simulation and Nigeria DHS data that ignoring jittering biases covariate effects and spatial range estimates.
- **Wu et al. (2024)**, *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series A: Statistics in Society*. Modelling urban/rural fractions in low- and middle-income countries. [doi:10.1093/jrsssa/qnae003](https://doi.org/10.1093/jrsssa/qnae003)  
  Proposes model-based estimation of urban/rural population fractions from gridded population data so that stratum-specific U5MR model estimates from DHS can be aggregated to administrative areas, and shows how these fractions affect subnational estimates and uncertainty.
- **Gascoigne et al. (2025)**, *Spatial and Spatio-temporal Epidemiology*. Estimating subnational under-five mortality rates using a spatio-temporal Age-Period-Cohort model. [doi:10.1016/j.sste.2024.100708](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sste.2024.100708)  
  Extends the smoothed-direct framework with an age-period-cohort structure plus spatial effects, separating birth-cohort from calendar-period effects in subnational U5MR estimated from DHS birth histories.
- **Li et al. (2025)**, *The R Journal*. Space-Time Smoothing of Survey Outcomes using the R Package SUMMER. [doi:10.32614/rj-2025-005](https://doi.org/10.32614/rj-2025-005)  
  Documents the SUMMER package: building person-month data from DHS birth recodes, computing weighted discrete-hazard direct estimates with jackknife variances, and fitting smoothed-direct and cluster-level space-time models via INLA.
- **Okonek et al. (2025)**, *Demographic Research*. A parametric survival model for child mortality using complex survey data. [doi:10.4054/demres.2025.53.26](https://doi.org/10.4054/demres.2025.53.26)  
  Proposes survey-weighted pseudo-likelihood estimation of parametric and nonparametric survival models for under-5 mortality from DHS birth histories, explicitly handling right censoring, left truncation for period-specific exposure and design weights, implemented in the R package pssst.
- **Paulson et al. (2026)**, *The Annals of Applied Statistics*. Temporal models for estimation and short-term forecasting of neonatal mortality rates in sub-Saharan Africa. [doi:10.1214/25-aoas2100](https://doi.org/10.1214/25-aoas2100)  
  Compares random-walk, AR, penalised-spline, natural-spline and logit-linear latent temporal models for neonatal mortality from DHS and MICS birth histories in 41 sub-Saharan countries, with out-of-sample validation and simulation showing RW2 and P-splines least biased.

## Data quality of birth histories

Omission, displacement, heaping, recall, AIDS bias, and validation against surveillance.

- **Potter et al. (1977)**, *Population Studies*. Problems in Using Birth-History Analysis to Estimate Trends in Fertility. [doi:10.2307/2173921](https://doi.org/10.2307/2173921)  
  Demonstrates that systematic misdating of births in retrospective birth histories (compression toward the survey date) plus omission produces spurious period trends when events are tabulated by calendar time.
- **Hill et al. (2006)**, *Demographic Research*. Neonatal mortality in the developing world. [doi:10.4054/demres.2006.14.18](https://doi.org/10.4054/demres.2006.14.18)  
  Reviews DHS neonatal mortality estimates, documenting heaping of ages at death at 7 days and 1 month, misreporting of early deaths as stillbirths, and the resulting instability of the neonatal share of infant deaths across surveys.
- **Hallett et al. (2010)**, *Bulletin of the World Health Organization*. Measuring and correcting biased child mortality statistics in countries with generalized epidemics of HIV infection. [doi:10.2471/blt.09.071779](https://doi.org/10.2471/blt.09.071779)  
  Uses an HIV transmission and survival model to quantify how deaths of HIV-positive mothers (whose children are omitted from birth histories) bias under-five mortality downward, and derives correction factors by HIV prevalence and time since the survey.
- **Guillot et al. (2012)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Child Mortality Estimation: A Global Overview of Infant and Child Mortality Age Patterns in Light of New Empirical Data. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001299](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001299)  
  Compares the infant-to-under-five mortality relationship implied by model life tables against DHS full-birth-history data across countries, showing that sub-Saharan African age patterns often deviate from historical model schedules and discussing reporting artefacts versus real epidemiological differences.
- **Silva et al. (2012)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Child Mortality Estimation: Consistency of Under-Five Mortality Rate Estimates Using Full Birth Histories and Summary Birth Histories. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001296](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001296)  
  Compares direct full-birth-history U5MR estimates with indirect summary-birth-history estimates across many DHS surveys and characterises the systematic under-estimation of FBH rates for periods 10-15+ years before the survey (recall/omission of distant deaths).
- **Walker et al. (2012)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Child Mortality Estimation: Methods Used to Adjust for Bias due to AIDS in Estimating Trends in Under-Five Mortality. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001298](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001298)  
  Formalises the UN IGME correction for downward bias in birth-history mortality estimates caused by the deaths of HIV-positive mothers before interview, deriving period- and survey-specific adjustment factors from modelled HIV prevalence.
- **Hill et al. (2015)**, *PLOS ONE*. Monitoring Change in Child Mortality through Household Surveys. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0137713](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0137713)  
  Assesses the consistency of under-five mortality estimates for overlapping periods from successive DHS/MICS surveys in the same countries, finding modest but systematic survey-to-survey differences and characterising the sampling and non-sampling error in FBH trend estimates.
- **Liu et al. (2016)**, *PLOS ONE*. Understanding Misclassification between Neonatal Deaths and Stillbirths: Empirical Evidence from Malawi. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0168743](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0168743)  
  Uses matched verbal-autopsy and survey data from Malawi to measure how often early neonatal deaths are reported as stillbirths (and vice versa) and which child, delivery and respondent characteristics predict misclassification.
- **Perez-Heydrich et al. (2016)**, *Spatial Demography*. Influence of Demographic and Health Survey Point Displacements on Raster-Based Analyses. [doi:10.1007/s40980-015-0013-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40980-015-0013-1)  
  Simulates over real DHS cluster locations how the 2 km urban / 5 km rural random displacement of GPS coordinates distorts raster-extracted covariate values and downstream regression coefficients, and evaluates buffer-averaging as a mitigation.
- **Verhulst et al. (2016)**, *Demographic Research*. Child mortality estimation: An assessment of summary birth history methods using microsimulation. [doi:10.4054/demres.2016.34.39](https://doi.org/10.4054/demres.2016.34.39)  
  Builds a microsimulation of fertility and mortality histories (including HIV and changing fertility) with known truth and uses it to measure the accuracy of Brass-type summary-birth-history estimators of under-5 mortality.
- **Warren et al. (2016)**, *Spatial Demography*. Influence of Demographic and Health Survey Point Displacements on Point-in-Polygon Analyses. [doi:10.1007/s40980-015-0015-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40980-015-0015-z)  
  Quantifies how DHS cluster displacement causes clusters to be assigned to the wrong administrative polygon and how that misclassification biases regression estimates that use polygon-level covariates.
- **Akuze et al. (2020)**, *The Lancet Global Health*. Randomised comparison of two household survey modules for measuring stillbirths and neonatal deaths in five countries: the Every Newborn-INDEPTH study. [doi:10.1016/s2214-109x(20)30044-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2214-109x(20)30044-9)  
  Cluster-randomised trial in five HDSS sites comparing a DHS-style full birth history plus pregnancy-loss module against a full pregnancy history; the pregnancy history captured more stillbirths and (at some sites) neonatal deaths, indicating under-capture of early neonatal deaths in the standard module.
- **Helleringer et al. (2020)**, *Demography*. Biases in Survey Estimates of Neonatal Mortality: Results From a Validation Study in Urban Areas of Guinea-Bissau. [doi:10.1007/s13524-020-00911-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-020-00911-6)  
  Record-links DHS-style full birth and full pregnancy histories to prospective Bandim HDSS data and shows that omission of neonatal deaths, stillbirth/early-neonatal misclassification and birth-date displacement bias survey neonatal mortality downward, with the pregnancy history performing somewhat better.
- **Eilerts et al. (2021)**, *Demographic Research*. Age patterns of under-5 mortality in sub-Saharan Africa during 1990–2018: A comparison of estimates from demographic surveillance with full birth histories and the historic record. [doi:10.4054/demres.2021.44.18](https://doi.org/10.4054/demres.2021.44.18)  
  Compares age-specific under-5 mortality from DHS full birth histories with INDEPTH HDSS sites in the same countries and with historical life tables, finding that DHS histories place a larger share of deaths in the 1-23 month range than surveillance data or the historic record.
- **Romero Prieto et al. (2021)**, *PLOS ONE*. Estimating the infant mortality rate from DHS birth histories in the presence of age heaping. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0259304](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0259304)  
  Documents heaping of reported age at death at 12 months in DHS birth histories and evaluates adjustment methods for the resulting misclassification of deaths across the infant/child boundary.
- **Guillot et al. (2022)**, *Demography*. Modeling Age Patterns of Under-5 Mortality: Results From a Log-Quadratic Model Applied to High-Quality Vital Registration Data. [doi:10.1215/00703370-9709538](https://doi.org/10.1215/00703370-9709538)  
  Fits a log-quadratic model to detailed age-specific under-5 mortality from high-quality vital registration to produce a reference age schedule (indexed by U5MR and one shape parameter) against which survey-derived age-at-death distributions can be compared or adjusted.

## Applied analyses with strong methods

Applied uses of DHS birth histories in good journals; the first block links mortality to malaria exposure.

- **Gemperli et al. (2004)**, *American Journal of Epidemiology*. Spatial Patterns of Infant Mortality in Mali: The Effect of Malaria Endemicity. [doi:10.1093/aje/kwh001](https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwh001)  
  Links Mali 1995-96 DHS birth histories to a Bayesian geostatistical map of malaria parasite prevalence and fits spatial logistic models for infant death adjusting for household and maternal covariates and between-cluster spatial correlation.
- **Kazembe et al. (2007)**, *Geospatial Health*. Spatial analysis of the relationship between early childhood mortality and malaria endemicity in Malawi. [doi:10.4081/gh.2007.253](https://doi.org/10.4081/gh.2007.253)  
  Links Malawi DHS 2000 birth histories to MARA malaria-risk surfaces and fits Bayesian geoadditive survival models (structured spatial effects plus nonlinear covariate smooths) to infant and child mortality, finding a positive but spatially heterogeneous association with endemicity.
- **Rajaratnam et al. (2010)**, *The Lancet*. Neonatal, postneonatal, childhood, and under-5 mortality for 187 countries, 1970–2010: a systematic analysis of progress towards Millennium Development Goal 4. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60703-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60703-9)  
  Estimates neonatal, post-neonatal, 1-4 year and under-5 mortality for 187 countries 1970-2010 by computing period-specific direct rates from full birth histories, applying new summary-birth-history methods, and synthesising all sources with Gaussian process regression on the log rate.
- **Bocquier et al. (2011)**, *Demography*. Is There an Urban Advantage in Child Survival in Sub-Saharan Africa? Evidence From 18 Countries in the 1990s. [doi:10.1007/s13524-011-0019-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-011-0019-2)  
  Pools DHS birth histories from 18 African countries into an event-history framework that separates age and calendar-period effects, compares adjusted urban and rural hazards, and discusses migration selection and recall in retrospective data.
- **Fink et al. (2011)**, *International Journal of Epidemiology*. The effect of water and sanitation on child health: evidence from the demographic and health surveys 1986–2007. [doi:10.1093/ije/dyr102](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyr102)  
  Pools 171 DHS surveys (births in the five years before each survey) and estimates household water/sanitation effects on child mortality and morbidity using survey fixed effects, design weights and cluster-robust standard errors.
- **Lim et al. (2011)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Net Benefits: A Multicountry Analysis of Observational Data Examining Associations between Insecticide-Treated Mosquito Nets and Health Outcomes. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001091](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001091)  
  Pools 29 DHS/MIS surveys to link household ITN ownership to child parasitaemia and to under-5 mortality from full birth histories (exposure matched to the year before survey), with survey fixed effects and stratification by MAP-derived transmission intensity, finding a 23% mortality reduction consistent with trials.
- **Alkema et al. (2012)**, *PLoS Medicine*. Child Mortality Estimation: A Comparison of UN IGME and IHME Estimates of Levels and Trends in Under-Five Mortality Rates and Deaths. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1001288](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1001288)  
  Systematically compares IGME and IHME under-5 mortality estimates for 2010 and finds that most discrepancies stem from different treatment of the same inputs (bias adjustments to summary birth histories, source weighting, smoothing and extrapolation) rather than from different underlying data.
- **Eisele et al. (2012)**, *The Lancet Infectious Diseases*. Malaria prevention in pregnancy, birthweight, and neonatal mortality: a meta-analysis of 32 national cross-sectional datasets in Africa. [doi:10.1016/S1473-3099(12)70222-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(12)70222-0)  
  Pools 32 DHS/MIS datasets to estimate the effect of IPTp and ITN exposure in pregnancy on low birthweight and neonatal mortality from birth histories, using exact matching on confounders and stratifying by MAP transmission intensity.
- **Kudamatsu et al. (2012)**, *Journal of the European Economic Association*. Has democratization reduced infant mortality in sub-Saharan Africa? Evidence from micro data. [doi:10.1111/j.1542-4774.2012.01092.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1542-4774.2012.01092.x)  
  Treats DHS full birth histories as a retrospective mother-level panel and identifies the effect of a calendar-time exposure (democratisation) on infant mortality with mother fixed effects, discussing recall error and selective survival of mothers over long retrospective windows.
- **Alkema et al. (2014)**, *PLoS ONE*. Child Mortality Estimation 2013: An Overview of Updates in Estimation Methods by the United Nations Inter-Agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0101112](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0101112)  
  Documents the IGME's 2013 methodological changes (the B3 Bayesian spline model) together with its data rules for birth-history series: which surveys and reference periods enter, how full versus summary birth histories are weighted, and exclusion criteria.
- **Dwyer-Lindgren et al. (2014)**, *Spatial and Spatio-temporal Epidemiology*. Estimation of district-level under-5 mortality in Zambia using birth history data, 1980–2010. [doi:10.1016/j.sste.2014.09.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sste.2014.09.002)  
  Combines full and summary birth histories from DHS surveys and censuses in a Bayesian space-time model to estimate district-level under-5 mortality in Zambia over three decades, with out-of-sample validation of the district trends.
- **Wang et al. (2014)**, *The Lancet*. Global, regional, and national levels of neonatal, infant, and under-5 mortality during 1990–2013: a systematic analysis for the Global Burden of Disease Study 2013. [doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60497-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60497-9)  
  Updates the IHME child-mortality pipeline with a two-stage model (covariate mixed-effects regression followed by Gaussian process regression), source-specific bias corrections for birth-history data, and separate age patterns for neonatal and infant mortality across 188 countries.
- **Golding et al. (2017)**, *The Lancet*. Mapping under-5 and neonatal mortality in Africa, 2000–15: a baseline analysis for the Sustainable Development Goals. [doi:10.1016/s0140-6736(17)31758-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0140-6736(17)31758-0)  
  Pools complete and summary birth histories from DHS, MICS and other surveys across Africa into a Bayesian geostatistical model of age-specific mortality by 5x5 km pixel and year, with covariates and calibration to national estimates.
- **Jakubowski et al. (2017)**, *PLOS Medicine*. The US President's Malaria Initiative and under-5 child mortality in sub-Saharan Africa: A difference-in-differences analysis. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1002319](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002319)  
  Builds child-level under-5 mortality outcomes from DHS/MIS birth histories across 32 countries (1995-2014) and estimates the effect of a country-year malaria-programme exposure with difference-in-differences models, country fixed effects and survey weights.
- **Burstein et al. (2019)**, *Nature*. Mapping 123 million neonatal, infant and child deaths between 2000 and 2017. [doi:10.1038/s41586-019-1545-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1545-0)  
  Extends the geostatistical mapping of age-specific child mortality from pooled birth histories to 99 LMICs at 5x5 km resolution for 2000-2017, combining age-bin-specific models with calibration to GBD national estimates.
- **Li et al. (2019)**, *PLOS ONE*. Changes in the spatial distribution of the under-five mortality rate: Small-area analysis of 122 DHS surveys in 262 subregions of 35 countries in Africa. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0210645](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0210645)  
  Applies the smoothed-direct space-time model to 122 DHS rounds across 35 African countries at admin-1, using survey random effects to absorb between-survey bias and validating subnational U5MR trajectories 1980-2015 against UN IGME national series.
- **Mejía-Guevara et al. (2019)**, *PLOS Medicine*. Age distribution, trends, and forecasts of under-5 mortality in 31 sub-Saharan African countries: A modeling study. [doi:10.1371/journal.pmed.1002757](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1002757)  
  Pools DHS full birth histories from 31 sub-Saharan African countries and uses Bayesian models to estimate and forecast mortality by month of age, documenting how the age distribution of under-5 deaths has shifted over time.
- **Papaioannou et al. (2019)**, *Scientific Reports*. Malaria-anemia comorbidity prevalence as a measure of malaria-related deaths in sub-Saharan Africa. [doi:10.1038/s41598-019-47614-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-47614-6)  
  Fits Bayesian geostatistical models of under-5 mortality from birth histories in 16 DHS/MIS surveys with cluster-level survey-measured parasitaemia and anaemia as covariates, and proposes malaria-anaemia comorbidity prevalence as a proxy for malaria-related mortality.
- **Verhulst et al. (2022)**, *The Lancet Global Health*. Divergent age patterns of under-5 mortality in south Asia and sub-Saharan Africa: a modelling study. [doi:10.1016/s2214-109x(22)00337-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2214-109x(22)00337-0)  
  Applies the log-quadratic age-pattern model to DHS full birth histories and HDSS data to show that sub-Saharan Africa has a distinctively high share of deaths after infancy compared with south Asia and historical schedules, after accounting for reporting artefacts.

## Reports (not peer-reviewed)

- **Pullum et al. (2014)**, *DHS Methodological Reports No. 11, ICF International (not peer-reviewed)*. Evidence of Omission and Displacement in DHS Birth Histories.  
  Screens birth histories from 192 DHS surveys since 1990 using indicators of omission of births and deaths and of displacement of birth dates and ages at death, reporting that omission of births is usually under 2% and of deaths usually under 5% but sometimes above 10%.

## Further additions from the completeness check

Confirmed on Crossref by a final reviewer as well-known work missing from the search; summaries not independently written.

- **Hobcraft et al. (1985)**, *Population Studies*. Demographic Determinants of Infant and Early Child Mortality: A Comparative Analysis. [doi:10.1080/0032472031000141576](https://doi.org/10.1080/0032472031000141576)  
  The classic cross-national analysis of World Fertility Survey birth histories: fits age-segment-specific hazard (logit) models for neonatal, post-neonatal, toddler and child mortality across dozens of countries, isolating birth interval, birth order, maternal age and sex effects.
- **Curtis et al. (1993)**, *Demography*. Birth interval and family effects on postneonatal mortality in Brazil. [doi:10.2307/2061861](https://doi.org/10.2307/2061861)  
  Fits multilevel discrete-time hazard models with mother-level random effects to the 1986 Brazil DHS birth histories and shows that unobserved family frailty accounts for a large part of the apparent short-birth-interval effect on post-neonatal mortality.
- **Bolstad et al. (2001)**, *Journal of the American Statistical Association*. Investigating Child Mortality in Malawi Using Family and Community Random Effects: A Bayesian Analysis. [doi:10.1198/016214501750332659](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214501750332659)  
  Fits a Bayesian hierarchical survival model to the 1992 Malawi DHS birth histories with nested family (mother) and community (cluster) random effects, using MCMC to separate unobserved family frailty from community-level heterogeneity in under-five hazards.
- **Kravdal et al. (2004)**, *Population Studies*. Child mortality in India: The community-level effect of education. [doi:10.1080/0032472042000213721](https://doi.org/10.1080/0032472042000213721)  
  Uses NFHS (India DHS) birth histories in multilevel discrete-time hazard models to estimate the effect of community-level (cluster-aggregated) education on child mortality net of individual-level covariates, discussing the identification of contextual effects from survey clusters.
- **Corsi et al. (2012)**, *International Journal of Epidemiology*. Demographic and health surveys: a profile. [doi:10.1093/ije/dys184](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dys184)  
  The IJE data-resource profile of the DHS programme: sampling design, questionnaire modules including the full birth history, recode file structure, weighting, GPS displacement, and known limitations for comparability across survey rounds.
- **Alkema et al. (2014)**, *The Lancet Global Health*. National, regional, and global sex ratios of infant, child, and under-5 mortality and identification of countries with outlying ratios: a systematic assessment. [doi:10.1016/s2214-109x(14)70280-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s2214-109x(14)70280-3)  
  Builds a Bayesian hierarchical time-series model for the sex ratio of infant, child and under-five mortality, using birth-history data from DHS and other surveys alongside vital registration, and identifies countries with outlying ratios suggestive of sex-selective mortality or reporting error.
- **Molitoris et al. (2019)**, *Demography*. When and Where Birth Spacing Matters for Child Survival: An International Comparison Using the DHS. [doi:10.1007/s13524-019-00798-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13524-019-00798-y)  
  Pools full birth histories from 77 DHS surveys and fits discrete-time hazard models with mother fixed effects and interactions by age segment and country development level, showing that the birth-interval effect is largest in high-mortality settings and concentrated in early ages.
- **Elkasabi et al. (2019)**, *PLOS ONE*. Calculating fertility and childhood mortality rates from survey data using the DHS.rates R package. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0216403](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216403)  
  Describes and validates the DHS.rates R package, which reproduces the DHS Program's synthetic-cohort direct estimation of neonatal, post-neonatal, infant, child and under-five mortality from birth histories, with jackknife standard errors that respect the survey design.
- **Rerimoi et al. (2019)**, *PLOS ONE*. Under-five mortality in The Gambia: Comparison of the results of the first demographic and health survey with those from existing inquiries. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0219919](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0219919)  
  Validates the 2013 Gambia DHS birth-history estimates of under-five mortality by age segment against HDSS and earlier survey and census data, finding good agreement for under-five rates but evidence of displacement and under-reporting of early deaths in the survey.
- **Wilson et al. (2020)**, *Biostatistics*. Pointless spatial modeling. [doi:10.1093/biostatistics/kxy041](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxy041)  
  Develops methods for geostatistical modelling of DHS outcomes when exact cluster coordinates are unavailable or jittered, integrating the spatial field over the area a cluster could lie in rather than treating displaced points as exact, and compares this with naive point-based analyses (online 2018, print 2020).

## Adjacent work dropped from the list but relevant to this project

These are not analyses of birth histories, which is why the reviewers excluded them, but they bear on linking modelled malaria prevalence to survey mortality.

- Subnational mapping of under-5 and neonatal mortality trends in India: the Global Burden of Disease Study 2000–17  
  Verified and sound, but it adds no birth-history methodology beyond Golding 2017 and Burstein 2019, is set in India rather than sub-Saharan Africa, and has no malaria dimension; cite Burstein instead.
- Adult Mortality From Sibling Survival Data: A Reappraisal of Selection Biases  
  Confirmed on Crossref and PubMed (Demography 50(1):207-228, Feb 2013; online 2012), but it is about sibling histories and adult mortality, not the analysis of birth histories. The survivor-selection logic is only loosely analogous to children of deceased mothers being missing from birth histories, so under a strict reading it is off-angle for this reader; cite only if a companion maternal-mortality series is added.
- Malaria mapping using transmission models: application to survey data from Mali  
  Exists (PubMed PMID 16357113, AJE 163(3), Feb 2006; Crossref lists 2005 as the online-first year). Sound paper, but its subject is exposure-surface construction, not analysis of birth-history data; keep only as background for defending PfPR2-10 age standardisation.
- Relation between severe malaria morbidity in children and level of Plasmodium falciparum transmission in Africa  
  Crossref-confirmed and important context for a possibly non-monotone transmission-mortality relationship, but it is hospital-admission data at five sites, not a birth-history analysis or a methods paper; cite in the discussion, not as methodological literature.
- Child mortality and malaria transmission intensity in Africa  
  Crossref-confirmed and the key counterweight to Snow 1997 on the all-cause dose-response, but it is an ecological site-level review in a commentary-style journal, not a DHS birth-history analysis; useful for comparing magnitudes, not for methods.
- Relationship between child survival and malaria transmission: an analysis of the malaria transmission intensity and mortality burden across Africa (MTIMBA) project data in Rufiji demographic surveillance system, Tanzania  
  Crossref-confirmed and relevant to the shape of the transmission-mortality dose-response, but it uses prospective demographic-surveillance data rather than DHS/MIS birth histories, so it is outside the birth-history methods scope; suitable only as a comparison for the estimated dose-response.
- Protective efficacy of interventions for preventing malaria mortality in children in Plasmodium falciparum endemic areas  
  Verified (PubMed 20348132), but it is a trial meta-analysis with no birth-history analysis; keep only as an external benchmark for triangulating a PfPR-derived attributable fraction, not as methods literature.
- The effect of malaria control on Plasmodium falciparum in Africa between 2000 and 2015  
  Verified (PubMed 26375008). Not a birth-history analysis; it documents the construction of the exposure surface (an earlier MAP generation) and should be cited in a measurement-error/circularity discussion of the PfPR covariate rather than as methods literature on birth histories.
- Mapping Plasmodium falciparum Mortality in Africa between 1990 and 2015  
  Verified (PubMed 27723434). Uses modelled all-cause envelopes, not DHS birth histories; relevant only as the model-based comparator against which an empirical DHS-based attributable-death estimate can be checked.
- Measurement error caused by spatial misalignment in environmental epidemiology  
  Verified (PubMed 18927119). Core statistical framework for using a smoothed MAP PfPR surface as a covariate (bias direction, understated variance, when two-stage plug-in is defensible), but its subject is air-pollution exposure, not birth-history data; cite as supporting methodology in a measurement-error section only.
- Efficient measurement error correction with spatially misaligned data  
  Verified (Crossref). Gives a practical route to propagating exposure-surface uncertainty (e.g. resampling MAP posterior draws) into the dose-response CIs, but it is not about birth-history analysis; supporting citation only.
- Health-exposure modeling and the ecological fallacy  
  Verified (Crossref; online 2005, print volume 7(3) 2006). Directly relevant when PfPR is averaged to region-survey units before attachment to a log-link count model with a smooth dose-response, but its subject is ecological regression generally, not birth histories; supporting citation only.
- The burden of malaria mortality among African children in the year 2000  
  Verified (PubMed 16507643). Added as a comparator only: it produces transmission-stratified malaria-attributable fractions from verbal autopsy, not from birth-history dose-response, so it is a benchmark for the AF results rather than methods literature.
- Mapping the global prevalence, incidence, and mortality of Plasmodium falciparum, 2000-17: a spatial and temporal modelling study  
  Verified (PubMed 31229234). Not birth-history analysis; the likely exposure surface actually used (a 2025 Lancet update to 2000-22 also exists: 10.1016/S0140-6736(25)00038-8). Cite for exposure provenance and uncertainty, alongside Bhatt 2015, in the measurement-error discussion.
- Global, regional, and national causes of under-5 mortality in 2000–15: an updated systematic analysis with implications for the Sustainable Development Goals  
  Confirmed in Crossref and peer-reviewed, but it is a cause-of-death allocation analysis based on verbal autopsy data and covariates, not an analysis of DHS/MIS birth histories; it may serve as an external comparator for malaria-attributable death counts but is out of scope for a methodological reading list on birth-history analysis.
